Meridian Health Co-op - Member Handbook Summary

Who We Are

Meridian Health Co-op is a member-owned network of 11 rural clinics located in Vermont and New Hampshire. The co-op was founded in 2011 when four independent rural clinics in the Upper Connecticut River Valley agreed to pool purchasing, share back-office staff, and jointly negotiate with insurers rather than each attempting to survive alone. Over the following decade seven additional clinics joined, bringing the network to its current size of 11 member clinics: six in Vermont and five in New Hampshire. Each member clinic keeps its own name, board, and local staff, but all members share the co-op's central services, its clinical programs, and its governance.

Meridian's central office is in White River Junction, Vermont, and employs a small shared-services team covering billing, information technology, quality reporting, and pharmacy purchasing. Clinical leadership for the network is provided by Chief Medical Officer Dr. Anika Sorensen, a family physician who practiced at one of the founding clinics for twelve years before taking the CMO role in 2019. Dr. Sorensen chairs the co-op's clinical quality committee and is responsible for the Lantern and Compass programs described below.

Governance and the Annual Member Assembly

Meridian is governed by a board made up of one representative from each member clinic, plus two at-large community members elected by the membership. The co-op holds an annual member assembly each October in Montpelier, Vermont. At the assembly, member clinics vote on the co-op's budget, elect board members, approve any new member clinics, and set the shared quality targets for the coming year. Attendance is required for at least one delegate from each clinic, and the assembly minutes are distributed to all clinic staff.

Financial Rules for Members

Each member clinic must keep at least 3 months of operating reserves in unrestricted funds at all times. This reserve rule was adopted at the 2014 assembly after one founding clinic nearly closed following a delayed insurer payment, and it is reviewed annually by the co-op's finance committee. A clinic that falls below the 3-month reserve requirement must file a recovery plan with the board within 60 days and may draw on the co-op's shared stabilization fund, which is capped at one month of that clinic's operating expenses. Member clinics also pay an annual dues assessment equal to 1.5 percent of net patient revenue, which funds the central office and the shared programs.

Telehealth Platform: Lantern

Meridian operates a shared telehealth platform called Lantern. Lantern lets patients at any member clinic connect by video or phone with a Meridian clinician for urgent-care questions, follow-up visits, behavioral health sessions, and medication reviews. The platform is staffed by a rotating pool of nurse practitioners, physicians, and licensed counselors drawn from the member clinics, so that no single small clinic has to provide after-hours coverage on its own. Lantern is available seven days a week from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m.

The average wait time for a Lantern visit is 9 minutes, measured from the moment a patient enters the virtual waiting room to the moment a clinician joins. Lantern handled roughly 38,000 visits in 2025, and Meridian estimates that about a third of those visits replaced an in-person urgent-care or emergency-department trip that would otherwise have required a long rural drive. Lantern visit notes flow directly into the patient's chart at their home clinic, and any Lantern clinician can schedule an in-person follow-up at the patient's home clinic.

Care Coordination Program: Compass

Compass is Meridian's care-coordination program for patients with complex or chronic conditions, especially those recently discharged from a hospital. Each member clinic has at least one Compass care coordinator, typically a registered nurse or community health worker, who contacts every discharged patient within 48 hours, reconciles medications, confirms follow-up appointments, and checks on home supports such as transportation and food access. Compass coordinators use a shared registry so that a patient who is seen at more than one member clinic has a single care plan.

Since Compass launched in 2017, the network's 30-day hospital readmission rate for enrolled patients has fallen from 17% to 11%. Dr. Anika Sorensen reviews the Compass readmission data quarterly with the clinical quality committee, and the readmission rate is one of the shared quality targets voted on at the October assembly. Compass also tracks emergency-department visits and patient-reported confidence in managing their own conditions.

Pharmacy Purchasing Pool: Meridian Rx

Meridian Rx is the co-op's pharmacy purchasing pool. By combining the drug and medical-supply purchasing of all 11 clinics into a single contract, Meridian Rx saves members an average of 22% compared with the prices individual clinics paid before joining the pool. The pool covers vaccines, common generic medications dispensed at clinic sites, point-of-care testing supplies, and durable medical supplies. Meridian Rx is managed by a pharmacist at the central office, who also maintains the co-op's shared formulary and coordinates with Compass coordinators on medication reconciliation after hospital discharge. Savings from Meridian Rx are returned to member clinics in proportion to their purchase volume.

How the Programs Fit Together

Meridian's model is built around the idea that small rural clinics can offer the services of a much larger system if they share what is expensive to do alone. Lantern spreads after-hours and specialty coverage across the network, Compass keeps recently hospitalized patients from returning to the hospital, Meridian Rx lowers the cost of supplies, and the 3-month reserve rule and the annual assembly keep each clinic financially and democratically accountable to the rest.
